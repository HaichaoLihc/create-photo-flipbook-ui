# Baseline Book-Editing Knowledge

Use this as reference, not as a workflow or house method. The selected photo skill or coherent combination of skills, the photographs, and the intended reading experience determine the edit. Adapt or ignore any heuristic below when the selected visual direction implies a different selection density, pairing logic, pace, use of text, or relationship to negative space.

Before editing, infer the selected skill set's editorial consequences. Decide whether it favors isolated images or groups, quiet or compression, literal continuity or association, repetition or disruption, and sparse or dense sequencing. If using multiple skills, give each a stable role and define what visually binds them. Let those decisions govern the book.

## Explore

- Read the contact sheet as a collection before selecting individual winners. Identify recurring subjects, gestures, colors, distances, moods, and absences.
- Notice the collection's range: quiet and loud, wide and intimate, human and unpeopled, light and dark, descriptive and ambiguous.
- Infer several plausible book directions, then choose the external photo skill whose visual grammar best reveals the strongest one. Revisit the initial reading after selecting the skill because it may change which photographs matter.

## Select

- Keep only photographs or finished artworks that strengthen the book. Technical adequacy is not enough.
- Curate after choosing the external photo skill. Select for both intrinsic strength and the editorial possibilities of its visual grammar; style fit can change which images are useful and how many the book needs.
- Prefer a short convincing edit over comprehensive coverage. Reject weak establishing shots, redundant views, and near-duplicates.
- Mix wide, medium, and detail views; human presence and absence; density and quiet; brightness and darkness when the collection supports them.
- Find one recurring condition, question, journey, or motif. Do not force a literal story when the images support association instead.

## Sequence and spread map

- Sequence through chronology when time matters; otherwise use subject, gesture, color, line, scale, atmosphere, contrast, or conceptual echo.
- Consider an opener, development, peaks, breathing room, and a resonant ending when they suit the chosen style; do not force a conventional arc.
- Use page turns to reveal, interrupt, compare, or reframe. Avoid explaining every transition.
- Let motifs recur with variation. Do not preserve filename order without editorial reason.
- Design the full sequence before generating. Keep the spread map internal; do not create a required plan or JSON artifact.
- Assign useful spread roles such as outside cover, opening, orientation, dialogue, transition, pause, acceleration, peak, echo, or ending. Invent different roles when the selected style needs them.
- Decide each spread's intended density, contrast, image scale, negative space, emotional temperature, human presence, visual weight, and transition from the previous spread.

## Pair and pace

- Pair through dialogue or productive contrast, not merely shared subject matter.
- Consider unequal pairings, single images facing quiet pages, and occasional blank leaves when compatible with the selected style.
- Vary density, scale, orientation, emotional temperature, and visual distance where that variation supports the selected style.
- Use supporting images as connective tissue. Not every image or spread needs to announce itself.
- Build change without randomness. Avoid long runs with the same image count, scale, density, or contrast; give a peak quieter neighbors and let pauses prepare the next turn.
- Do not impose a mechanical alternation. Let the photographs determine where rhythm accelerates, holds, breaks, and resolves.

## Arrange

- Generate the outside cover as the first full spread: back cover on the left, front cover on the right, with an intentional spine transition.
- Derive the structural vocabulary from the selected style. Common options include one image on a page, one image across a spread, two images in dialogue, a small related cluster, and blank pauses.
- When a photo skill generates a double-page spread, preserve it as one composition and divide it only at its intended gutter.
- Keep output dimensions and ratio consistent across the book. Add a blank leaf when correct spread pairing requires it.
- Use the selected visual system for the complete outside-cover spread. Let the back cover remain quieter than, but materially and chromatically continuous with, the front.
- Preserve the relevant selected skills' visual invariants while varying their adaptable compositional variables to serve each spread's role.

## Critique

Reject a book when:

- it includes most inputs merely to increase page count;
- repetition does not develop meaning or rhythm;
- several adjacent pages share the same subject, scale, density, or emotional temperature;
- pairings duplicate information rather than create dialogue;
- the opener explains too much or the ending merely exhausts the folder;
- a strong image is weakened by unnecessary companions;
- the selected photo skills appear as unrelated one-off treatments rather than one visual family;
- spreads repeat one layout recipe or remain uniformly dense, loud, sparse, or high-contrast;
- variation feels arbitrary rather than developing a legible rhythm across page turns;
- a gutter damages an important subject or generated spread;
- page order, artwork order, or visual continuity is unclear at contact-sheet scale.
