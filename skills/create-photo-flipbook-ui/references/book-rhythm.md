# Rhythm across the book

A photobook unfolds in time. The spread is a moment; the turn is an edit. Design anticipation, interruption, accumulation, pause, and return so that a strong image gains meaning from its neighbors. A book can hold a sustained quiet or dense passage, but constant maximum intensity makes individual peaks indistinguishable.

## Edit before decorating

Choose an emotional or visual proposition from the photographs. Sequence relationships rather than merely dividing photographs into topics: a gesture answered by a landform, a close detail following a distant scene, a repeated object changing scale, a quiet aftermath following motion. Leave some connections open for the reader.

Select enough space for the story to breathe. Do not force every good photo into the edit, fill every leaf, or make every spread a self-contained promotional poster. An image may be powerful because it arrives late, appears small, or is followed by silence.

Save `sequence-plan.md` before generating. A compact table is enough. For each spread record:

- Role in the emotional arc and source images.
- Left/right relationship and the intended effect of the following turn.
- Overall intensity and occupied area, including a genuinely quiet passage and a deliberate peak.
- Text presence, exact words, scale, voice, and placement; explicitly mark text-free spreads.
- Image scale/count/treatment and color coverage/saturation.
- Which motifs, patterns, objects, lines, and shapes appear, change, or stay absent.

Use descriptions or rough low/medium/high levels to expose repetition, not to compute an aesthetic score. The plan should reveal changes when read as a sequence. Avoid mechanically alternating loud/quiet or changing everything merely for variety.

## Compose several rhythms at once

| Element | Useful changes across the sequence | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text | silence → small handwritten note → a brief emphasis → silence; changing amount, position, line length | a large headline on every spread, uniformly tiny notes, compulsory numbering or slogans |
| Images | distant/close, one/many, small/immersive, cropped/isolated, photographic/abstract | identical image footprint, every subject blown up, every spread full bleed |
| Color | neutral or low-chroma resting state, one accent, a selected saturated event, release | the whole palette on every page, constant cobalt/vermilion fields by habit |
| Motifs and objects | introduce, echo at another scale, withdraw, return transformed | logo-like repetition, the same cutout pasted throughout |
| Pattern and texture | dense repetition, sparse trace, smooth silence | coarse halftones or paper distress applied at equal strength everywhere |
| Lines and shapes | thin/thick, short/extended, directional/still, filled/open, absent/present | generic diagonal marks, disks, or borders repeated without purpose |
| Space | occupied/open, compressed/expansive, balanced/displaced | all empty space confined to the same corner or margin |

These rhythms need not peak together. A large image can be muted and text-free; a tiny bright object can command a mostly empty spread; an intimate note can follow a dense collage. Let one or two elements lead at a time. Avoid routinely combining giant text, saturated color, dense imagery, heavy patterns, and multiple motifs in the same spread.

## Page turns and visual memory

- Establish a few visual constants, then vary how strongly they are present. Keep the reader oriented without making the next spread predictable.
- Use asymmetry within a spread and across turns. A detail near the right edge can anticipate the next scene; a later echo can recall an earlier shape with changed meaning.
- Let impact passages earn their intensity through quieter neighbors. Breathing room can be open paper, a single small detail, muted imagery, a nearly empty leaf, or a simple image with no words or decorative marks.
- Vary the duration of pauses and accumulations. A quiet sequence may last more than one spread; a peak can arrive abruptly. Do not reduce pacing to an alternating template.
- Let the closing passage release or transform an earlier idea. It need not be another headline, visual climax, or miniature catalogue of everything used.

## Review the sequence, then revise

Before extending the book, generate an impact spread and a quiet spread from the plan. Compare them at reader size and as thumbnails. If both have large words, broad saturated fields, or comparable density, revise the relevant prompt and regenerate before using either as a book-wide reference.

Review all accepted artworks in order on a contact sheet and through the reader. Also inspect individual left/right leaves: a quiet whole spread can conceal two equally crowded pages, and mobile viewing changes what is seen together.

Ask where the eye slows, where it accelerates, what returns, and what is deliberately withheld. At thumbnail size, do repeated giant text blocks or color fields flatten the arc? Is the quietest passage noticeably quieter in several dimensions? Are motifs evolving rather than accumulating? Do transitions follow the content rather than a decorative formula?

Save a brief `rhythm-review.md` describing the actual arc, any intended-versus-generated differences, and revisions made. Regenerate spreads that flatten the sequence. Individual beauty does not excuse a monotonous book; variation must be visible in the delivered artwork, not only stated in the plan.
