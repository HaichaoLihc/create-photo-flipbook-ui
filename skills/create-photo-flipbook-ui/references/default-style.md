# Default editorial artist-book aesthetic

Make a personal, photo-led artist book with tactile material, expressive source transformations, casual handwritten words, and an authored sequence. Generalize the zine references' freedom and intimacy; let each new collection determine its story and visual language. The reader should feel changes of attention and tempo as pages turn.

Read [book rhythm](book-rhythm.md) before planning. Cohesion comes from a few recurring decisions; the amount and intensity of those decisions can change dramatically.

## Photographs and composition

- Freely crop, cut out, fragment, recolor, redraw, or abstract the source material. Mix photographic, illustrated, and graphic passages as the book requires. Strong art direction can be as quiet as one small transformed detail on an otherwise empty leaf.
- Give each spread a visual lead, then decide what can disappear. Vary image scale, count, crop, placement, edge treatment, and occupied area. Use close detail, distant view, a small sequence, an isolated object, and occasional immersive imagery when they advance the edit.
- Compose the two leaves together and consider the next turn. A busy left leaf can release into a nearly empty right leaf; an edge, gesture, or color can set up an echo after the turn. Protect critical text and faces from the fold.
- Avoid a recurring template of giant headline plus full-bleed duotone image. Allow text-free imagery, small images, open paper, and asymmetric intervals to carry equal artistic weight.

## Color, material, and marks

- Derive a restrained palette from the collection. Paper and a dark or soft ink establish a resting state; accent color can appear, recede, or disappear. Use broad saturation and strong color collisions as selected events. A shared palette does not mean every color appears on every spread.
- Judge perceived color load, including image colors, colored text, fills, and motifs together. A full-bleed photograph can be quiet when low-contrast; a small high-chroma mark can supply all the emphasis a spread needs.
- Apply paper grain, halftones, photocopy edges, washes, or torn scraps selectively. Dense texture contributes visual noise. Quiet passages may need clean paper, soft edges, or barely visible material instead of the same coarse grain everywhere.
- Derive motifs, objects, patterns, lines, and shapes from the photographs. Give each a role in the sequence: introduction, transformation, absence, return. Vary its scale, frequency, placement, and weight rather than stamping it on every page. A line may direct a gaze; a shape may become a container; neither needs to be present without a purpose.
- Tickets, maps, receipts, stamps, and documentary notes require supplied or supported material. Do not decorate unrelated images with invented travel evidence.

## Typography and words

- **Prefer casual handwritten text as the default expressive voice.** Think personal pencil or felt-tip notes, natural spacing, a light irregular baseline, and readable, unforced letterforms. Choose a consistent hand suitable for the language. Avoid turning the preference into oversized brush lettering, graffiti, or a different novelty hand on every page.
- Most words can be small, intimate annotations or brief phrases. Some spreads need no text. Use medium lettering for a transition and reserve large lettering for a deliberate emphasis. A display title on the cover does not establish the scale of all interior text.
- A restrained serif, sans, or monospaced companion can support factual captions, folios, and colophon. Use it sparingly; do not let a universal condensed headline style dominate the book.
- Vary text presence, amount, scale, placement, line length, and relation to the image. A fragment close to an object feels different from a note across open paper. Empty space around words helps control their volume. Keep small text readable at the final reader size.
- Write from visible observations and user-provided information. Dates, places, names, quotations, and personal memories require support. Short poetic language is welcome when it does not pretend to document an invented experience. Avoid obligatory slogans or chapter labels on every spread.

## Continuity with variation

Choose only the book's essential constants: perhaps its paper, handwriting, and one source-derived motif. Let scale, composition, color coverage, text presence, and texture fluctuate around them. A quiet spread must actually reduce visual demand; leaving a blank corner beside huge type and dense halftones does not create a full pause.
