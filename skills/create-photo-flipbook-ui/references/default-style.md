# Default editorial photo-zine principles

Make an expressive photo zine with a strong editorial point of view and a visible artistic language. Combine recognizable photography with purposeful collage, graphic interpretation, drawing, or print treatment. A zine can be polished or raw, restrained or exuberant; torn edges, distressed paper, tape, and handwriting are not genre requirements. The default should feel edited, designed, and artistically interpreted.

Use [creative ideation](creative-ideation.md) to choose the direction, then [book rhythm](book-rhythm.md) to develop it over page turns. Keep the creative range of an artist book while giving the reader a distinctly editorial experience.

## Develop image treatment and page composition together

The unit of design is a pair of pages. Give the leaves roles: one establishes a photograph while the other questions it, several details build toward a larger frame, or a typographic passage interrupts an image sequence. The gutter can separate, compare, withhold, or connect. It is more than a line to keep faces away from.

Keep enough photographic specificity for the sources to remain present, while allowing their edges, scale, color, material, and relationship to the page to change. Rectangles, hard crops, cutout silhouettes, open edges, and layered fragments are all tools. Intact photographs can provide grounding and rest. Their availability should not make untouched rectangles on flat color the book's entire visual language.

Use several page structures where the material calls for them, with enough recurring alignment and type logic to feel like one publication. Possibilities include:

- An unequal diptych whose two frames share a gesture or contradict each other.
- A tight run of frames or details that makes the reader compare and move through time.
- A photographic block facing a short observation, a graphic interpretation, or a differently scaled image.
- A deliberately cropped contact sequence, a diagram-like arrangement, or a source fragment crossing a clear page boundary.
- A typographic interruption or a nearly empty leaf that changes the next image's force.
- An immersive full-bleed photograph or montage at a moment that needs that scale.

These are possibilities, not a rotation to complete. Give the chosen structures the book's artistic treatment rather than stopping at image placement. Full bleed can be powerful, but a book dominated by uninterrupted scenes across both leaves becomes an image portfolio. Changing a template's background color or the number of photo boxes does not establish an artistic language either.

## Choose a small artistic vocabulary and vary its strength

Develop selection, pairing, crop, scale, repetition, and sequence alongside a few expressive operations grounded in the source. Usually one or two related operations can sustain a book: selective overprinting that picks up a source color; a cut photographic edge continuing as drawing; a source silhouette becoming a mask or ink field; a pattern extracted from a detail; or a photographic fragment interrupted by a deliberately different material. Choose them for what they express and evolve their role across turns. A decorative shape added beside an otherwise unchanged layout is not enough if it creates no relationship.

Use a range of intervention within this shared language:

- **Light:** an intact or tightly cropped photograph, perhaps with a restrained echo of the book's material or mark. Let selected pauses remain almost untouched.
- **Mixed:** recognizable photography interacts with a transformed detail, cutout, overprint, drawing, mask, or graphic extension. This is the useful center of the default: the photographic and artistic layers both carry meaning. One image can remain natural while another changes, or only part of a single image may be transformed.
- **Strong:** a bolder collage, radical scale shift, extensive abstraction, or illustrated passage earns a particular moment in the arc. Preserve enough source connection to keep it part of this book.

These are degrees of intervention, not quotas or a repeating light/mixed/strong cycle. Give mixed passages enough sustained presence to establish the style. A dramatic cover followed by untouched photo layouts does not meet that aim. Conversely, every photograph does not need an effect. Visual intensity is a separate decision: a tiny contour transformation can be quiet; an unaltered crowd photograph can be loud.

Collage should retain meaningful friction between its components: a hard cut, an overlapping print, a scale discontinuity, or two deliberately separate viewpoints. Images can cross margins, overlap, or open into marks when purposeful. Avoid routinely smoothing everything into one atmospheric, perspectivally continuous scene, and avoid treating every image edge as an untouchable box.

Preserve the source's peculiar details rather than replacing them with generalized beautiful scenery. Ask what remains recognizably photographic and what gains expressive force through intervention. Apply stricter fidelity or greater transformation when the user requests it.

## Give typography an editorial job

Plan type with the images. It can establish an opening, make a comparison, interrupt a rhythm, connect dispersed fragments, or provide a personal voice. Decide its alignment, measure, scale, and spacing as part of the composition. A cover title and occasional microscopic epigram are not a complete typographic idea.

Choose one primary family or consistent handwriting style for the whole book: a precise grotesk, an intimate hand, a particular serif, a cut-letter construction, or another approach the concept supports. Define stable roles for the text that actually exists, such as title, observation, caption, and colophon, with their relative sizes, weights, case, spacing, and alignment logic. Add a second family or hand only when a necessary function cannot be served by the primary one; record that function and use it consistently. A change of scene or a desire for variety is not enough reason.

Adapt typography to each scene through placement, scale within the defined hierarchy, line breaks, contrast, and its relationship to the images. Keep the letterforms and voice recognizable across cover and interiors. A typographic interruption can use the same family at a different scale. Make small words readable at the final reader size. Avoid both a large headline on every spread and text so timid that it has no compositional presence.

Wordless sequences remain valid; their image arrangement must carry the editorial structure. Folios, labels, and captions are useful when they organize the publication; they are not compulsory ornaments.

## Write one connected text

Develop the words alongside the image sequence and edit them together, from title to closing line. Use one theme or question, a consistent point of view and register, and a coherent tense where relevant. Each passage should advance, complicate, recall, or gently redirect that thread. Consistency does not mean repeating the theme in every caption. Some turns should remain wordless.

Write as someone paying attention to these particular photographs. Prefer concrete observations, natural syntax, precise nouns and verbs, or the user's own phrasing. Allow wit, understatement, fragments, and poetry when they suit the established voice. Avoid a succession of interchangeable lyrical slogans, abstract profundity, repeated sentence formulas, or a grand conclusion the images have not earned. Human warmth comes from specificity and judgment; do not manufacture it with invented memories, forced slang, or deliberate mistakes.

Dates, places, names, quotations, and personal experiences need support. Use first-person memories only when the user has supplied them or requested fiction; do not infer feelings or events from a photograph. Do not invent copy to fill a typographic shape. If a phrase adds neither information, perspective, nor a meaningful connection, leave the space quiet.

Read the complete copy aloud in order before generation. Does it sound like one person with something particular to say? Do references and transitions make sense? Could a line be moved to an unrelated travel book unchanged? Revise generic, redundant, or disconnected lines, then record the exact words in the sequence plan. Check the rendered text against that copy and reread it as a whole after generation.

## Color, material, and detail

- Choose page grounds and color relationships from the collection and concept. Dark ink, saturated fields, clean white, toned stock, and photographic areas can all belong. A ground is a designed surface; it need not become a sky or atmospheric backdrop.
- Establish a few recurring spatial and material decisions: an image alignment, a family of margins, an ink treatment, or a type relationship. Break them for an editorial reason. Let local richness develop around that structure rather than introducing a new medium on every turn.
- Treat grain, overprint, misregistration, cut edges, and drawing as specific production choices. Apply them locally and purposefully; a uniform texture filter adds finish without solving the composition.
- Derive motifs from the sources and let them evolve, disappear, or change function. An interval, crop rule, or recurring juxtaposition can be a motif as much as a drawn shape.
- Use supplied or supported tickets, receipts, maps, stamps, and documentary notes only when they matter to the edit. Invented ephemera does not make a book more like a zine.

## Rest and aesthetic judgment

Quiet can be a small unaltered photograph, a modest pair, a few well-spaced words, a smooth colored field, or one low-contrast image. Vary the kind of rest. Repeating a tiny extracted object on a huge empty field creates another formula, even when its color changes.

Judge precision, tension, surprise, hierarchy, and economy. Inspect how image edges align or intentionally miss, how type meets the page, how the eye moves between fragments, and what is gained by the next turn. Remove elements that only advertise effort. More cutouts, more texture, or more surreal imagery do not automatically raise the aesthetic standard.

Before accepting a sequence, test both limits. If the design amounts to photo boxes, a background fill, and captions, strengthen the source-derived artistic operations and their interaction with the page. If photographic character disappears into continuous paintings on every turn, retain more photographic material and reduce the intervention in selected passages. The ordinary middle spreads should show the intended balance, with recognizable photographs and visible artistic decisions. Quiet passages can be plainer; they should feel like rests inside an established visual language.
